Artificial life and evolutionary development

Life World is a spatial model of organisms whose bodies, brains and ecological roles develop from inherited genomes.

Each organism is diploid. Its genome holds a developmental program, a brain and a set of morphological and physiological traits. A body grows from one cell by that program; the brain turns what it senses into turning and thrust; the body sets what it can eat, how fast it swims, how well it fights and what it costs to run. Organisms that gather enough energy breed, their genomes recombine and mutate, and lineages evolve.

What they become is left to evolution. The founders are a three-cell grazer. Predators, armour, eyes, photosynthesis, new body plans and longer developmental programs appear only if they evolve.

The companion Genomic World project studies population genetics and climate adaptation. Its test suite compares drift, mutation-selection balance, linkage decay, selection and fixation with theory.

Pixels from the genome

The default view draws the world straight from the grids the simulation keeps: each world cell takes its colour from its algae, detritus, flow and terrain, and each body cell is a square of its type's colour, laid out where the developmental program put it. Single cells are rasterised from their morphological genes. The frame is drawn at half size and scaled up by the GPU, so a full-screen frame takes about 12 ms. V switches to the smooth natural, bright-field and dark-field styles.

Three ways to build an organism

Body plan

Cells of eight types grown from a program of cell-identity genes: mouths, motors, eyes, armour, spikes, stores, photosynthetic cells. Genes duplicate and diverge, so the number of cell kinds is open.

Morphology

One cell built as a protist is. Size, shape, pigment, flagella, cilia, spines, membrane, eyespot, cytostome, raptorial organelles and chlorophyll are genes, and they set both how it looks and what it can do.

Combined, in 3D

The multicellular body, each cell sized, tinted and made better or worse at its job by the morphological genes. Bodies can grow in two dimensions or three.

2D and 3D views

The 3D view ray-marches the terrain so ridges and dish walls stand up, and draws every body cell as a lit sphere. The camera orbits and follows.

Experimental evolution and microbial ecology

The open ocean has gyres around ridges, upwelling that drifts, day and night. The laboratory presets are simplified versions of published experiments, so a run can be compared with the reported patterns. Interactive browser models let readers explore selected mechanisms.

Range expansion on agar

Two neutral strains spread from a drop. Cells hardly move on agar, so the colony grows at its edge, where few cells found each new patch.

Hallatschek, Hersen, Ramanathan and Nelson (2007), Genetic drift at expanding frontiers promotes gene segregation. PNAS 104: 19926.

The strains separate into sectors at the edge by drift alone: local heterozygosity falls to 0.06 of the global value, against 0.94 in a stirred culture.

MEGA-plate

A long plate with antibiotic in bands rising toward the middle, inoculated at both ends, under bright-field.

Baym, Lieberman, Chase, Gross and Kishony (2016), Spatiotemporal microbial evolution on antibiotic landscapes. Science 353: 1147.

Mean resistance reaches 0.39 with the drug and stays at 0.003 without it, over 5 replicates; every drug replicate reaches the highest band.

Predator and prey, with a refuge

A raptorial predator and its prey in a small dish, in dark-field. In one version, sediment at the left edge hides prey.

Gause (1934), The Struggle for Existence.

Over 8 replicates, prey persist with the refuge in every dish (about 2,200 each). Without it, prey and predator both die out in 2 dishes, as in Gause's, prey die out alone in 2, and a remnant of prey holds on in 4.

Phototaxis on a slide

A slide lit from one end, on poor medium. Cells can sense the light gradient along their heading.

Cells gather toward the lamp (mean position 158 against 128 under even light). Blinding the light sense changes nothing. The gathering comes from growth where light is strong; steering adds nothing measurable.

Open ocean

A 2D incompressible flow drives food and drifters around ridges and through passes; current strength cycles and the upwelling moves, so what a body needs keeps changing.

Predators appear within 2,000 ticks and settle between 2 and 16 percent of the population.

Petri dish, and serial transfer

Liquid culture under a lamp. A serial-transfer version moves a tenth of the population to fresh medium every 600 ticks.

Population genetics and climate adaptation

Genomic World simulates individuals in space and tracks ecological and evolutionary change. Diploid populations forage, reproduce and evolve on a 256 by 256 torus with a latitudinal temperature gradient. Each genome carries 128 loci across four chromosomes, with recombination and mutation.

The model tracks allele frequencies, marker heterozygosity, regional FST, adaptation to local temperature, heritability, dispersal and genetic load. Its experiments vary mutation rate, climate, population bottlenecks and mountain passes.

Read the Genomic World source and run instructions on GitHub.

How it works

  • Genome. 640 fixed loci per haplotype on twenty chromosomes, recombining by Haldane's map, plus a developmental program of cell-identity genes. Programs cross over at most once per meiosis, sometimes misaligned by a gene, which duplicates or deletes one, as unequal crossing-over does.
  • Development. Bodies grow cell by cell in 2D or 3D and are mirrored across the midline. A gene that grows its own identity repeats a segment; a timer stops it. Random programs grow bodies of up to 8,965 cells in tests.
  • Brain. A fixed genetic core (ten senses, four self-feeding hidden neurons, turning and thrust) and an evolved network on top: one gene per neuron, with its activation (tanh, logistic, rectifier or sine), bias, self-connection, three incoming connections and its pull on the outputs. Neurons are inserted, duplicated, deleted and rewired, so the network's size and wiring evolve. Founders have none. In a 30,000-tick ocean run the mean network grew to 11.6 neurons, the largest to 18. A control with inert networks grows them just as large, so this growth is mutation pressure.
  • Ecology. Grazing on algae that regrow; photosynthesis that shares light and draws nutrient; detritus that is scavenged and returned to the water as nutrient; predation by attack against defence; toxin and the cost of resisting it.
  • Water. Stam's stable fluids on a 128 by 128 grid with walls; organisms drift with the current and swim against drag that depends on body shape.
  • Determinism. Every random draw is a hash of an organism's seed, the tick and a purpose, so runs give the same state on any number of threads.
  • Viewer. A software renderer in Flow, shown full screen through Core Animation at the panel's native resolution, with CoreText type. Natural colour, bright-field and dark-field styles. An inspector shows a followed organism's brain at work and its developmental program gene by gene.

Run it

Needs Flow 2.0 with the flowc C backend, a C11 compiler, and macOS for the window and the renderer's typography. Homebrew libomp makes the parallel loops use every core.

./run.sh life         the life world, full screen
./run.sh life-bench   headless: forms, trophic roles, energy, selection
./run.sh shot         headless renders (settings in life_shot.flow)
./run.sh test         tests, determinism, validation against theory

Limits

  • The experiments are simplified. The organisms are model cells, rates are scaled for speed, and a tick is not calibrated to any species.
  • Bodies are bounded only by a scratch grid (31 cells each way in 2D, 15 in 3D) and by energetics; the developmental program holds up to 48 genes per haplotype in the memory set aside for it.
  • The world is two-dimensional; 3D bodies swim in a plane.
  • Fitness emerges from an energy budget with hand-set constants. No selection coefficient is a parameter; selection is measured, parents against the population.
  • The window and the typography are macOS only for now.
